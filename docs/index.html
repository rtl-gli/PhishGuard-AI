The idea

A practical project with a real problem behind it.

Phishing affects people every day. I wanted to make something more useful than a notebook: a small web tool that shows its reasoning and gives someone a sensible next step.

01

Look at the link

The model checks 16 details, including length, domain structure, HTTPS, redirects, and unusual symbols.

02

Show the reasoning

The result includes the decision path, so the score is not just a mysterious number.

03

Keep it useful

The interface gives practical advice and never asks for passwords or personal information.

What I measured

Numbers matter, but context matters too.

I compared a baseline logistic regression model with decision trees and tested different tree depths. The final model was chosen from those experiments, not picked at random.

85.11%Test accuracy
84.49%Phishing F1 score
81.14%Phishing recall
16URL features

PhishGuard AI is an educational estimate, not a guarantee. A low-risk result does not mean a link is definitely safe.

What I learned

The difficult part is not only the model.

Building this made me think about false negatives, readable explanations, and how to design for someone who may already be worried. A prediction is only useful if a person can understand what to do with it.

Current limitation

This is a URL-pattern detector, not a guarantee of safety. It does not visit a website or replace advice from your bank, email provider, or security team.
